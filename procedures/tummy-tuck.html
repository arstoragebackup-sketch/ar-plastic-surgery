Tummy Tuck (Abdominoplasty) in Karimnagar

Pregnancy and major weight loss can leave abdominal skin and muscles that no amount of exercise will restore. A tummy tuck at AR Plastic Surgery removes excess skin, addresses separated muscles, and recreates a firmer abdominal contour.

Overview

Abdominoplasty, commonly called a tummy tuck, removes excess skin and fat from the abdomen and tightens the underlying abdominal muscles, which often separate during pregnancy or stretch with weight gain. Unlike liposuction, which removes fat alone, a tummy tuck addresses loose skin and muscle laxity together. The navel is typically repositioned to suit the new contour. The result is a smoother abdominal profile that clothing fits more comfortably over. Muscle repair also restores a sense of core support many patients notice in daily movement.

It suits adults at a stable weight who have loose, overhanging abdominal skin, a persistent bulge from separated muscles, or both — most commonly after pregnancy or significant weight loss. Candidates should be in good health, finished with planned pregnancies, and clear that this is contouring surgery, not a weight-loss method. Non-smokers heal more reliably. A stable routine of diet and activity before surgery supports both safety and the quality of the result.

Consultation assesses skin excess, muscle separation, fat distribution, and any hernias or previous abdominal scars that affect planning. Dr Reddy reviews your weight history and medical background, and examines whether liposuction alone, a full tummy tuck, or a limited procedure is the right fit for your anatomy. If further weight loss is advisable first, or if your goals are modest enough for a lesser procedure, he will say so. Previous scars and any abdominal wall weakness are mapped carefully, since they shape the surgical plan.

Honest expectations include a long, low scar placed to hide beneath clothing — it fades but does not vanish — and several weeks of restricted activity. The result is a flatter, firmer abdomen, but future pregnancy or major weight gain can undo the improvement. Stretch marks below the navel are often removed with the excised skin; those above it usually remain. Maintaining your weight after surgery is what protects the improvement over the years. Results vary from person to person.

Our approach

Dr Reddy plans the excision and muscle repair around your anatomy: separated muscles are stitched back together to restore core firmness, excess skin is removed in a measured amount, and the remaining skin is redraped smoothly. Liposuction of the flanks is sometimes combined for balanced contour. The navel is recreated with attention to natural appearance.

Safety and candour guide the plan. He will not operate until your weight is stable, and he declines when expectations — such as a scar-free result — cannot be met. Drains, compression, and staged activity are used to support healing, and follow-up visits monitor progress closely, because the early weeks of recovery determine much of the final quality.

What to expect

Consultation

You will discuss your history — pregnancies, weight changes, previous abdominal surgery — and what bothers you about your abdomen. Dr Reddy examines skin laxity, muscle separation, and fat, sometimes asking you to tense your abdomen to demonstrate the separation. You will learn which procedure fits, where the scar will lie, and what recovery demands, with honest advice if the timing is not right.

The procedure

A tummy tuck is performed under general anaesthesia. An incision is placed low across the abdomen, separated muscles are repaired, and excess skin and fat are removed before the remaining skin is redraped and closed. The navel is repositioned. Drains are usually placed temporarily. The operation takes several hours, and you are monitored closely during early recovery. You will wake with compression and drains in place, and nursing staff will help you sit and walk the same day.

Recovery

The first weeks require real rest: walking bent slightly, avoiding strain, and wearing compression as directed. Drains are removed when output falls. Desk work is often possible within a couple of weeks, while exercise and lifting wait for clearance. Swelling lingers for weeks to months, and the final contour — and scar maturation — is judged over the long term.

Risks and limitations — stated honestly

Every procedure carries trade-offs. Before you decide, Dr Ashok Reddy will discuss these with you directly:

  • Scarring: the incision typically runs hip to hip, placed low to hide under clothing. It fades considerably but remains visible, and individual scar quality varies.
  • Fluid collection: fluid can accumulate beneath the abdominal skin despite drains, occasionally needing drainage in clinic. Compression and limited early activity reduce this risk.
  • Wound healing issues: areas of delayed healing can occur, particularly in smokers or patients with medical conditions affecting circulation. Honest pre-operative assessment aims to minimise this.
  • Changes in sensation: numbness of the lower abdominal skin is common after surgery and improves gradually, though some altered sensation may persist long-term.

FAQ

Common questions

What is the difference between a tummy tuck and liposuction?

Liposuction removes fat but does nothing for loose skin or separated muscles; a tummy tuck removes excess skin and repairs the muscle layer. If your concern is pinchable fat with firm skin, liposuction may suffice. If skin hangs or muscles have separated — common after pregnancy — only a tummy tuck addresses it. Dr Reddy examines you and recommends honestly.

Can separated abdominal muscles be repaired?

Yes — muscle separation, often called diastasis, is stitched back together during a tummy tuck, restoring firmness to the abdominal wall. This is one of the main reasons the procedure helps post-pregnancy bulges that exercise cannot fix. The repair is a structural part of the operation, not an optional extra.

Will a tummy tuck remove my stretch marks?

Stretch marks located on the skin that is removed — usually below the navel — go with it. Marks above the navel typically remain, though they may sit lower after redraping. A tummy tuck is not a stretch-mark treatment as such; their partial removal is a side benefit. Dr Reddy shows you honestly what to expect.

How long is recovery after a tummy tuck?

Expect several weeks of restricted activity, with walking encouraged early and strain avoided. Desk work is often possible within a couple of weeks; exercise, lifting, and core strain wait for medical clearance at follow-up. Swelling persists for weeks to months. Because healing varies, Dr Reddy clears each stage individually rather than by a fixed calendar.

Will I need drains after surgery?

Usually, yes — small drains prevent fluid build-up beneath the abdominal skin during early healing and are removed once output falls, often within the first week or two. They are a routine, temporary part of recovery. Dr Reddy explains their care before discharge and monitors them at follow-up visits.
