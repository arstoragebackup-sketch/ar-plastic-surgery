PRP Therapy in Karimnagar

PRP therapy uses growth factors from your own blood to support hair and skin health. At AR Plastic Surgery, Dr Ashok Reddy offers PRP as an honest, evidence-aware option — helpful for the right patients, never oversold.

Overview

PRP — platelet-rich plasma — is prepared from a small sample of your own blood, which is processed to concentrate the platelets and their growth factors. The concentrate is then injected into the scalp for thinning hair or into the skin for rejuvenation. Because it uses your own blood, the risk of allergic reaction is minimal. Treatment is given as a series of sessions rather than a single visit. The entire process uses sterile, single-use equipment for safety.

PRP suits people with early-stage hair thinning who want to support existing hair, patients complementing a hair transplant, or those seeking modest skin quality improvement. It is not a substitute for transplantation in established baldness, and it cannot create follicles where none remain. Candidates should have realistic expectations and be willing to commit to the recommended series of sessions. It is also sometimes used to support healing skin after other aesthetic procedures.

At consultation Dr Reddy assesses your pattern and stage of hair loss or your skin concerns, reviews blood-related conditions and medications such as blood thinners, and discusses honestly whether PRP is likely to help you. If your thinning is too advanced for PRP to matter, or if another treatment would serve better, he will redirect you rather than sell sessions. Your expectations and willingness to commit to the full series are discussed openly.

Honest expectations are crucial because PRP results are gradual and variable — some patients notice meaningful improvement in hair density or skin texture, others see little change. It supports and maintains more than it transforms. Maintenance sessions are usually needed to sustain any benefit. Dr Reddy presents PRP as one tool among several, chosen only when it genuinely fits your situation. Photographs taken over the series help judge honestly whether the treatment is helping.

Our approach

Dr Reddy's approach starts with selection: PRP is offered only where the biology makes sense — living follicles to support, skin with realistic room for improvement. Preparation follows standard protocols for concentrating platelets, and injections are placed methodically across the treatment area. The process is unhurried, with attention to comfort and sterile technique throughout.

What distinguishes his practice is restraint in a field full of hype. He will not promise regrowth PRP cannot deliver, and he tracks your response across sessions so treatment continues only while it is plausibly helping. If progress stalls, he says so and discusses alternatives — including accepting that no further treatment is the right answer.

What to expect

Consultation

You will discuss your hair thinning or skin concerns, how long they have persisted, and what you have tried. Dr Reddy examines the affected areas, reviews your medical history and medications, and explains what PRP can and cannot do for your specific situation. You will learn how many sessions are typically recommended, what each involves, and what honest expectations look like.

The procedure

A small amount of blood is drawn — similar to a routine blood test — and processed to concentrate the platelets. The scalp or skin is cleansed and numbed, and the PRP is injected in a grid pattern across the treatment area. The session takes under an hour, and you return to normal activities the same day with simple aftercare guidance.

Recovery

There is minimal downtime: mild soreness, redness, or pinpoint marks at injection sites settle within a day or two. Hair-related results, if they come, develop gradually over months as follicles respond — there is nothing to see in the first weeks. Follow-up sessions are spaced over months, with progress reviewed honestly at each visit. There are no bandages and no time off work needed after a session.

Risks and limitations — stated honestly

Every procedure carries trade-offs. Before you decide, Dr Ashok Reddy will discuss these with you directly:

  • Pain and bruising: injections into the scalp or skin cause soreness and occasional bruising. Numbing keeps this manageable, and it settles quickly in most patients.
  • Infection: any injection carries a small infection risk. Sterile technique and proper preparation keep this risk low, and aftercare guidance supports clean healing.
  • Headache: some patients experience a mild headache after scalp injections, usually brief and responsive to simple measures. It is mentioned so it does not cause alarm.
  • No noticeable benefit: the most honest risk is that PRP may not produce visible improvement for you. Individual response varies widely, which is why expectations are set carefully beforehand.

FAQ

Common questions

What exactly is PRP?

PRP stands for platelet-rich plasma: a concentrate of your own blood's platelets, which carry growth factors involved in healing. A small blood sample is drawn and processed to separate and concentrate these platelets, and the resulting plasma is injected where support is needed — typically the scalp for thinning hair or the skin for rejuvenation.

Can PRP regrow hair on a bald scalp?

No — PRP cannot create new follicles where none exist, and it is not a substitute for transplantation in established baldness. Its role is supporting existing, weakened follicles in early thinning, or complementing a transplant. Dr Reddy is direct about this limit: if your loss is too advanced, he will tell you PRP is not worth pursuing.

How many PRP sessions are needed?

PRP is given as a series of sessions spaced over months, not a single treatment — follicles and skin respond gradually. The exact plan depends on your condition and response. Dr Reddy reviews progress at each visit and continues only while treatment appears to be helping, rather than selling a fixed package regardless of outcome.

Is PRP safe since it uses my own blood?

Using your own blood eliminates allergy risk and keeps the safety profile favourable, but PRP is still a medical procedure requiring sterile technique and proper assessment. Blood disorders, certain medications, and active scalp infections may rule it out. A proper consultation ensures it is appropriate for you specifically.

Should I choose PRP or a hair transplant?

They address different stages: PRP supports existing hair in early thinning, while transplantation redistributes follicles to areas of established loss. They are sometimes combined. The right choice depends on your pattern, stage, donor supply, and goals — which is exactly what Dr Reddy assesses at consultation before recommending either, both, or neither.
