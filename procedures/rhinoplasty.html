Rhinoplasty in Karimnagar

The nose sits at the centre of the face, so even small changes alter overall balance. Rhinoplasty at AR Plastic Surgery reshapes the nose with restraint — refining rather than reinventing — under the care of Dr Ashok Reddy.

Overview

Rhinoplasty is surgery that reshapes the nose by modifying bone, cartilage, and sometimes skin and soft tissue. It can reduce a dorsal hump, refine a wide or bulbous tip, straighten asymmetry, or narrow the bridge, and it can also address internal structures that obstruct breathing. The operation is performed through incisions inside the nostrils or a small incision across the columella, chosen according to what needs to change. Each nose is assessed as a whole, since the bridge, tip, and nostrils influence one another.

Good candidates are adults whose facial growth is complete — usually from the late teens onward — who have a specific, well-considered concern about their nose and realistic ideas about change. It suits people seeking better facial balance rather than perfection, and those with breathing difficulties linked to structural issues. Patients should be in good health, non-smokers or willing to stop, and psychologically prepared for a gradual result. Patients who have lived with their concern for years and thought it through tend to be the most satisfied.

Consultation involves a detailed discussion of what bothers you, examination of the nose inside and out, and assessment of skin thickness, which influences how much refinement shows. Dr Reddy evaluates breathing as well as appearance, since the two are connected. If your expectations exceed what your anatomy allows, or if the concern is minor enough that surgery's trade-offs outweigh benefits, he will counsel against operating. Bringing reference photographs can help the discussion, though no surgeon can replicate another person's nose on your face.

Honest expectations are essential because the nose heals slowly. Swelling — especially at the tip — settles over many months, and the final shape emerges gradually rather than revealing itself when bandages come off. The aim is a nose that suits your face and looks like it always belonged there, not a different nose entirely. Protecting the nose from injury during the healing months is important, as the structures remain delicate. Minor asymmetries are normal in every nose, and results vary from person to person.

Our approach

Dr Reddy plans rhinoplasty around preservation and balance: supporting structures are respected so the nose remains strong and functional, not just smaller. Changes to the bridge, tip, and nostrils are considered together, since altering one area affects the others. Where breathing is impaired, functional correction is integrated into the aesthetic plan rather than treated as an afterthought.

Restraint defines his approach. He will talk you out of changes that would not suit your face, and he declines surgery when motivations are impulsive or expectations are unachievable. The procedure is performed with meticulous technique to control bleeding and protect the airway, and aftercare guidance covers splints, activity limits, and the long timeline of swelling resolution — so you know what to expect at every stage.

What to expect

Consultation

You will explain what you dislike about your nose and what you hope to achieve, while Dr Reddy examines its structure, skin, and your breathing. Photographs help the discussion, and he will explain which changes are achievable and which are not. Your medical history is reviewed, and you will hear honestly about risks, the long healing timeline, and whether surgery is advisable at all.

The procedure

Rhinoplasty is performed under anaesthesia, with incisions placed inside the nostrils or across the columella depending on the plan. Bone and cartilage are carefully reshaped, and functional issues such as a deviated septum are corrected as needed. A splint protects the new shape during early healing. The operation typically takes a few hours, after which you are monitored as you recover from anaesthesia.

Recovery

A splint is usually worn for about a week, with bruising around the eyes that fades gradually. Most people return to routine activities within a couple of weeks, avoiding contact sports and heavy exertion for longer as advised. Nasal stuffiness is common early on. Crucially, tip swelling resolves over many months, so the final result is judged at a year, not at a month.

Risks and limitations — stated honestly

Every procedure carries trade-offs. Before you decide, Dr Ashok Reddy will discuss these with you directly:

  • Prolonged swelling and healing: the nasal tip can remain swollen for many months, and early asymmetry often reflects uneven swelling rather than the final result. Patience during this phase is necessary.
  • Breathing changes: reshaping the nose can occasionally affect airflow, for better or worse. Functional assessment before and after surgery aims to protect and, where possible, improve breathing.
  • Need for revision: rhinoplasty is among the most demanding aesthetic operations, and a small number of patients seek refinement later. Dr Reddy discusses this possibility openly at consultation.
  • Visible scarring and skin issues: the columellar scar in open rhinoplasty usually heals discreetly, but thick or oily skin can limit refinement and prolong swelling, which is assessed beforehand.

FAQ

Common questions

Will rhinoplasty affect my breathing?

It should not worsen it when properly planned — and when a deviated septum or valve issue contributes to your concerns, correcting it can improve breathing. Dr Reddy examines your airway as part of every rhinoplasty consultation, because form and function are inseparable in the nose. Any pre-existing breathing difficulty is factored into the surgical plan.

What is the difference between open and closed rhinoplasty?

Closed rhinoplasty uses incisions hidden inside the nostrils, while open rhinoplasty adds a small incision across the columella for greater visibility of the cartilage framework. The choice depends on the complexity of the changes needed. Dr Reddy recommends the approach that gives the safest, most precise result for your particular nose.

How long does swelling last after rhinoplasty?

Longer than most patients expect. While the splint comes off within a week or two and much swelling fades over the first months, subtle tip swelling can persist for up to a year. This is normal healing, not a complication. Final judgement of the result waits until the tissues have fully settled.

Can rhinoplasty fix a crooked nose?

Often, yes — deviation caused by bone or cartilage can be straightened, and associated breathing issues addressed at the same time. However, severe deviation, previous injury, or asymmetric facial growth can limit how straight the nose can become. Dr Reddy assesses the cause of the crookedness and gives an honest estimate of achievable improvement.

What if I am unhappy with the result?

First, allow full healing — many early concerns resolve as swelling settles over months. If a genuine issue remains after about a year, revision options are discussed honestly, including what can and cannot be improved. Choosing a surgeon who plans conservatively and communicates clearly greatly reduces the likelihood of disappointment.
