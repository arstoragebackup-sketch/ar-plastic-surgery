Laser Treatments in Karimnagar

From unwanted hair to pigmentation and scars, lasers treat skin concerns with focused light energy. At AR Plastic Surgery, laser treatments are selected and supervised by Dr Ashok Reddy, matched to your skin type and concern.

Overview

Laser treatments use focused light energy to target specific structures in the skin: hair follicles for long-term hair reduction, pigment for spots and melasma, blood vessels for redness, or water in skin cells for resurfacing and scar improvement. Different concerns need different lasers and settings, which is why the device and its operator matter as much as the technology's name. A thorough consultation matches the right device and settings to your specific concern.

Lasers suit adults with unwanted hair, pigmentation concerns, acne scars, or uneven texture who understand that results come gradually across multiple sessions. Suitability depends heavily on skin type and hair colour — for example, laser hair reduction works most effectively on dark hair, and tanned or very dark skin needs cautious settings. A test patch is often the sensible first step. Realistic expectations and commitment to the full series are as important as the technology itself.

At consultation Dr Reddy examines your skin type, the concern to be treated, your sun exposure, and your history of pigmentation issues or keloid scarring. He determines whether laser is appropriate, which type suits you, and what settings are safe — or whether a peel, medical treatment, or no treatment is the better answer. He will not treat skin that laser would endanger. Medications that increase sun sensitivity are reviewed, since they affect treatment safety.

Honest expectations: lasers improve gradually over a series — hair reduction, for instance, needs multiple sessions timed to hair growth cycles, and aims for long-term reduction rather than the removal of every single hair. Pigmentation may need maintenance as sun exposure continues. Protective eyewear, test patches, and sun discipline are non-negotiable parts of safe treatment. Skipping sun protection between sessions is the most common reason results disappoint. Results vary from person to person.

Our approach

Dr Reddy's approach is conservative and skin-first: settings start cautiously, test patches precede full treatment where indicated, and the plan respects your skin type's pigmentation risk. Each session's parameters are recorded and adjusted based on your response. The goal is the safest effective result, not the most aggressive treatment the skin can theoretically tolerate.

He is frank about what lasers cannot do: they cannot safely treat every skin type for every concern, they do not replace sun protection, and they are not a shortcut around realistic session counts. If your concern would respond better to a peel or medical therapy, he says so. Safety protocols — eyewear, cooling, aftercare — are followed without exception.

What to expect

Consultation

You will discuss your concern — unwanted hair, pigmentation, scars, or texture — and your skin's history with sun, pigmentation, and scarring. Dr Reddy examines your skin type, explains which laser suits your case, and outlines how many sessions are typically needed and what improvement to expect. A test patch may be done to confirm your skin responds safely. You will leave knowing the planned number of sessions and the spacing between them.

The procedure

Treatment areas are cleansed, protective eyewear is worn, and the laser is applied in passes over the skin — most patients feel warmth or a snapping sensation, managed with cooling. Sessions range from minutes for small areas to longer for larger ones. Soothing care is applied afterward, with sun protection emphasised before you leave. Larger areas like the legs take longer, while the upper lip takes only minutes.

Recovery

Mild redness and warmth resembling sunburn are common for hours to a day; treated pigmentation may darken before fading. Hair sheds gradually over the following weeks rather than falling out immediately. Sun avoidance and protection are essential between sessions. Each session builds on the last, with progress reviewed before the next is scheduled. Patience through the series is rewarded, as each session builds on the last.

Risks and limitations — stated honestly

Every procedure carries trade-offs. Before you decide, Dr Ashok Reddy will discuss these with you directly:

  • Burns and blisters: excessive energy or unsuitable skin can burn. Conservative settings, test patches, and proper cooling are the safeguards, which is why operator skill matters.
  • Pigmentation changes: lasers can darken or lighten treated skin, especially in darker skin types or with sun exposure. Careful patient selection and sun discipline reduce this risk.
  • Scarring: uncommon with appropriate settings but possible, particularly with aggressive treatment or poor aftercare. This risk is discussed honestly before treatment begins.
  • Incomplete response: some hair, pigmentation, or scarring responds less than hoped despite correct treatment. Realistic expectations and a test patch help identify this early.

FAQ

Common questions

Is laser hair removal long-lasting?

No treatment removes every hair forever. Laser hair reduction aims for long-term, substantial reduction — most patients see a major decrease in growth, with occasional maintenance sessions for regrowth over the years. Anyone promising complete permanence is overselling. Dr Reddy explains realistic outcomes, including that hormonal changes can stimulate new growth later.

How many laser sessions will I need?

Multiple — hair grows in cycles and only active-phase follicles respond, so sessions are spaced weeks apart to catch successive cycles. Pigmentation and scars likewise improve gradually. The number depends on your concern, skin, and response. Dr Reddy reviews progress across sessions and stops when further treatment offers diminishing returns.

Does laser treatment hurt?

Most patients describe warmth or a rubber-band snap sensation, brief and tolerable, with cooling used for comfort. Sensitivity varies by area and individual. Numbing options exist for sensitive zones. It is uncomfortable rather than painful for most people, and sessions are short.

Is laser safe for darker skin tones?

It can be, with the right laser type and conservative settings — but darker skin carries higher risk of pigmentation changes and burns, so device choice and operator expertise matter enormously. Dr Reddy assesses your skin type carefully, uses appropriate technology and test patches, and will decline treatment when the risk outweighs the benefit.

Laser treatment or chemical peel — which is better?

Neither is universally better; they suit different concerns. Lasers target specific structures like hair follicles or deep pigment with precision, while peels improve surface texture and superficial pigmentation more broadly. Sometimes they complement each other. Dr Reddy evaluates your skin and recommends the approach — or combination — most likely to help.
