10 Questions to Ask Before Your Hair Transplant

A hair transplant is a medical procedure, not a haircut. The quality of your result is decided long before the day of surgery, in the planning.

By Dr Ashok Reddy, MBBS, DNB (Mumbai), M.Ch (Delhi), Gold Medalist — Plastic, Cosmetic, Hand & Microvascular Surgeon

Am I a Suitable Candidate?

Not everyone with hair loss is ready for a transplant. The pattern and stability of your loss matter: if hair is still shedding rapidly, operating too early can leave an unnatural result as surrounding hair continues to thin. A careful surgeon assesses your age, family pattern and the likely course of your loss before advising surgery.

Your donor area, usually the back and sides of the scalp, is the second half of the equation. Its density and quality set the limit on what can be achieved. An honest assessment measures this area rather than guessing, because transplanted hair can only ever be redistributed, never created.

What Will My Personal Plan Look Like?

Ask what density is realistic for you, not in general. A good plan balances coverage with conservation: using too much donor hair in one session can leave nothing in reserve if you need a second procedure later. The design should suit your face, your age and the way you are likely to look in ten or twenty years.

Hairline design deserves its own discussion. A natural hairline is irregular, age-appropriate and built around the direction of your native hair. Templates and one-size-fits-all hairlines are a warning sign; your hairline should be drawn for you, on you, with your input.

Who Designs the Procedure, and Why It Matters

This is the question many patients never think to ask. In many clinics the planning is delegated, but the decisions that shape your result, where the hairline sits, how grafts are distributed, how the donor area is protected, are surgical decisions. They deserve a surgeon's judgement.

At AR Plastic Surgery, transplant planning is surgeon-led. Dr Ashok Reddy's background in plastic and microsurgery brings a tissue-handling discipline to every graft: careful extraction, minimal time outside the body and precise placement. Technique matters, but planning matters more.

What Happens on the Day?

A transplant day is long, often several hours, and it should feel calm and organised. Ask how the day is structured, what anaesthesia is used for the donor and recipient areas, and how the team monitors grafts between extraction and placement.

Also ask who does what. Trained assistants play a legitimate supporting role in most clinics, but the critical steps, planning, hairline design, donor harvesting strategy and supervision throughout, should remain under the surgeon's direct control.

What Should I Expect Afterwards?

Transplanted hair commonly sheds in the weeks after surgery before new growth begins; this is a normal part of the cycle and not a sign of failure. Visible improvement builds gradually over many months, and results vary from person to person.

Ask about aftercare instructions, follow-up visits and what ongoing care your native hair needs, since medical therapy for existing loss is often part of the long-term plan. A clinic that plans your follow-up as carefully as your surgery is thinking about your result, not just your booking.

FAQ

Common questions

Am I too young for a hair transplant?

There is no single cut-off age, but operating while hair loss is still aggressive can cause problems later. A surgeon will assess the stability of your loss, your donor area and your family pattern before advising whether the timing is right.

Will the transplanted hair fall out again?

Transplanted hair usually sheds within weeks of surgery and then regrows over the following months. Because it comes from areas less affected by pattern loss, it generally behaves like donor hair, though results vary and native hair may continue to thin.

How many sessions will I need?

That depends on your degree of loss, donor supply and goals. Some patients achieve their aim in one well-planned session, while others need a staged approach over time. A surgeon-led plan will lay this out honestly at the start.

Does planning really matter more than technique?

Both matter, but planning decides the ceiling of your result. Hairline design, graft distribution and donor conservation are judgement calls that shape how natural your outcome looks for years, and they cannot be fixed easily afterwards.
