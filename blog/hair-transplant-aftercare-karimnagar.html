Hair Transplant Aftercare in Karimnagar: Recovery, Do's and Don'ts

Short answer: a hair transplant only succeeds if the grafts survive the first two weeks. That means no touching, scratching or rubbing the recipient area, gentle washing exactly as instructed, sleeping with the head raised, and no gym, swimming, sun or tight headwear until your surgeon clears you. Shedding in the first weeks is normal, and new growth typically appears from around three to four months, getting fuller over six to twelve months. This guide covers day-by-day aftercare, what is normal, and the warning signs that need a call to the clinic.

The first 48 hours matter most

Fresh grafts are fragile. In the first two days, do not touch, scratch or rub the transplanted area, and do not let anything press against it — including a pillow pressed flat on the grafts, a tight cap, or a helmet. Sleep on your back with your head raised on two pillows, or as your surgeon advises.

Some swelling of the forehead or around the eyes is common in the first few days and usually settles on its own. Mild soreness in the donor area is also normal. Take only the medicines your surgeon prescribed, and do not start or stop any regular medicine without asking.

  • Do not touch or scratch the grafts
  • Sleep on your back, head raised
  • Avoid bending over sharply or lifting heavy weights
  • Do not wash your hair until your surgeon says to
  • Keep the area dry and uncovered where possible

Washing your hair after a transplant

Most surgeons advise a first gentle wash after two to three days, but follow your own surgeon's timing — it varies with the technique used. Use lukewarm water, a mild shampoo, and a very gentle touch: let water and lather run over the area rather than rubbing it.

For the first ten to fourteen days, do not rub, scrub or pick at scabs, and do not use a strong shower jet directly on the grafts. Pat dry with a soft towel or let the hair air-dry. After the scabs have cleared and your surgeon confirms it, you can return to normal washing gradually.

Sleeping and positioning

Sleep on your back with your head raised for the first week, or for as long as your surgeon advises. This reduces swelling and stops the grafts rubbing against the pillow. A travel pillow can help you stay in position through the night.

Avoid sleeping face down or on your side with the transplanted area pressed into the pillow until cleared. If swelling appears around the eyes, it usually peaks around day three or four and then fades.

What to avoid in the first weeks

  • No gym, heavy exercise, running or heavy lifting for at least two weeks, or as advised — sweating and strain can dislodge grafts and increase swelling
  • No swimming, saunas or steam rooms until cleared, because of infection risk
  • No direct sun on the scalp for the first few weeks; wear a loose, surgeon-approved cover outdoors if needed
  • No hats, caps or helmets pressing on the grafts until cleared
  • No scratching or picking at scabs, even when itching is strong
  • No smoking and no alcohol in the early healing period — both slow healing
  • No hair dye, chemical treatments or harsh styling products until your surgeon clears them

Shedding and regrowth: the normal timeline

Between about two and eight weeks after surgery, the transplanted hairs usually shed. This is called shock loss, and it is normal — the hair shaft falls out while the follicle stays in place and rests. It does not mean the transplant has failed.

New growth typically starts around three to four months, appearing fine at first and thickening over time. The result generally keeps improving through six to twelve months, and sometimes longer. Growth speed and density vary from person to person, and your surgeon can judge progress at follow-up visits rather than by daily comparison in the mirror.

Itching, scabs and redness: what is normal

Itching as scabs form is common and usually fades as they clear. Do not scratch — dab gently or use only what your surgeon recommends. Redness in the recipient area typically fades over a few weeks, and small scabs usually clear within ten to fourteen days.

Call the clinic promptly for spreading redness, increasing pain, pus, bad smell, fever, heavy bleeding, or swelling that keeps worsening after the first few days. These can signal infection or another problem that needs early treatment.

Medicines and follow-up visits

Take prescribed medicines exactly as directed and attend every follow-up, even if everything feels fine — the surgeon checks graft survival, donor healing and early growth. Do not start hair-loss medicines such as finasteride or minoxidil on your own after surgery unless your surgeon advises them; they suit some patients and not others, and timing matters.

Bring a list of all medicines and supplements you take to each visit, and tell the clinic about any new scalp treatment, dye or product before you use it.

Protecting the result long term

Once healed, treat the transplanted hair like normal hair, but keep protecting the scalp from strong sun, and avoid repeated harsh chemical treatments. A healthy diet, not smoking, and managing ongoing hair loss with your surgeon's advice help preserve the overall result, since non-transplanted hair can continue to thin over time.

If hair loss progresses in untreated areas, discuss options at follow-up rather than assuming another procedure is needed — the plan depends on your donor supply, hair loss pattern and goals.

Common questions

When can I wash my hair after a hair transplant?

Most surgeons advise a first gentle wash after two to three days, using lukewarm water and mild shampoo without rubbing the grafts. Follow your own surgeon's timing, as it varies with technique.

Is it normal for transplanted hair to fall out?

Yes. Shedding between about two and eight weeks — shock loss — is normal. The hair shaft falls while the follicle stays and rests; new growth typically starts around three to four months.

When can I go back to work?

Desk work is often possible within a few days, once swelling and soreness allow. Jobs involving dust, heat, helmets or heavy lifting need longer — ask your surgeon for your specific case.

When can I exercise or go swimming?

Light walking is usually fine early on, but gym, heavy lifting, running and swimming are typically avoided for at least two weeks, and swimming until the scalp has fully healed and your surgeon clears you.

When will I see the final result?

New growth typically starts at three to four months and keeps improving through six to twelve months. Speed and density vary between patients, so progress is judged at follow-up, not day by day.

When should I call the clinic urgently?

For spreading redness, increasing pain, pus or bad smell, fever, heavy bleeding, or swelling that keeps worsening after the first few days. Early treatment matters for infection.
