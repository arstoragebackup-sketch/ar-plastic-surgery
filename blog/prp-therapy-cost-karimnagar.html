How PRP per-session pricing works

PRP (platelet-rich plasma) therapy uses a small sample of your own blood, processed to concentrate the platelets, which is then injected into the treatment area — the scalp for hair concerns, or the face for skin concerns. Each visit is one session, and each session is priced within the same indicative band. There is no single flat fee for "PRP treatment" as a whole, because PRP is planned as a course: a number of sessions spaced weeks apart, with the count agreed after examination.

That is why two people can pay different totals for PRP at the same clinic. One person may need a short course for one area; another may need a longer course, or treatment for more than one area. The per-session figure stays within the same band — it is the number of sessions that moves the total.

6 factors that decide your price

  1. Number of sessions. The single biggest factor — the total is the per-session rate times the sessions in your course.
  2. Area treated. Hair and face/skin sit in the same band, but the area and the number of injection points move where you sit within it.
  3. Your individual plan. What is being treated and how you respond shape how many sessions are planned.
  4. Who performs the treatment. A qualified, experienced doctor's assessment and technique are part of what you pay for.
  5. Preparation and clinic standards. Proper blood processing, sterile technique and single-use materials all sit inside a proper quote.
  6. Follow-up and review. Whether review visits between sessions are included in the quote.

What's usually included — and what to ask

A proper written quote should itemise: the consultation and examination, the blood draw and PRP preparation for each session, the injections for the agreed area, and any scheduled review visits. Before you commit at any clinic, ask:

  • Who will perform the treatment — the doctor or someone else?
  • How many sessions are planned, and what does the total cover?
  • Which area is included in this quote?
  • Is preparation and are materials included, or charged separately?
  • Are review visits between sessions included?
  • Can I see the quote in writing before the first session?

Red flags in very low quotes

If a quote is far below the market range, slow down and check: is a qualified doctor actually performing the treatment, or is it delegated? Is the preparation done properly with sterile, single-use materials? Does the quote cover the full planned course, or will later sessions appear as extra charges? Is any review included at all? Price matters, but this is a medical procedure using your own blood — the cheapest option is not automatically the safest one.
