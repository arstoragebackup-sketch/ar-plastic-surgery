Hand Injury First Aid: What to Do in the First Hour

A cut, crush or fracture in the hand is different from the same injury elsewhere. What you do before reaching medical care can protect tendons, nerves and future function.

By Dr Ashok Reddy, MBBS, DNB (Mumbai), M.Ch (Delhi), Gold Medalist — Plastic, Cosmetic, Hand & Microvascular Surgeon

Stay Calm and Assess Quickly

In the shock of the moment, pause for ten seconds and look. Is there heavy bleeding? Can the person move all their fingers? Is anything visibly deformed or deeply cut? A quick assessment tells you whether this is a clinic visit or an emergency.

Keep the injured hand still and, if possible, raised. Remove rings promptly, since swelling can trap them within minutes. Speak calmly to the injured person; panic makes everything, including bleeding, worse. If the person feels faint, have them sit or lie down before you treat the hand.

Control the Bleeding

Press firmly on the wound with a clean cloth or bandage and maintain steady pressure; this controls most hand bleeding. Elevate the hand above heart level while pressing, since gravity is a genuine ally here.

If blood soaks through, add more cloth on top rather than removing the first layer. Keep pressing until help arrives or bleeding clearly stops. For most cuts, sustained direct pressure works; what fails is pressure that is too light or released too soon.

What NOT to Do

Do not apply turmeric, toothpaste, oil or other home substances to an open wound; they contaminate tissue and complicate proper cleaning later. Do not try to push a deformed finger straight, pull out an embedded object, or probe a deep cut to see how bad it is.

Do not tie a tight cloth or rope around the wrist as a tourniquet unless bleeding is life-threatening and will not stop with pressure; cutting off circulation can damage the very tissues you are trying to save. And do not delay care because the cut looks small; in the hand, small surface wounds can hide divided tendons or nerves.

When to Rush to a Hand Surgeon

Seek a hand surgeon urgently for deep cuts, especially on the palm side; numbness or loss of movement in any finger; visible deformity; fingertip injuries; crush injuries; or wounds that will not stop bleeding. These are the injuries where specialist repair in the early hours protects long-term function.

When in doubt, get the hand assessed the same day; an unnecessary visit costs little, but a missed tendon or nerve injury can limit hand function long-term.

While waiting for care, keep the hand elevated and avoid using it. If a dressing is already in place and bleeding is controlled, leave it undisturbed; repeated peeking restarts bleeding and introduces contamination.

FAQ

Common questions

Should I wash a deep cut before reaching the hospital?

Rinse lightly with clean water if the wound is dirty, then cover it with a clean cloth and apply pressure. Avoid scrubbing, antiseptics inside deep wounds or home remedies; thorough cleaning is safest under medical care.

What if a fingertip is cut off?

Control bleeding with pressure, wrap the severed part in a clean moist cloth, place it in a sealed bag and keep that bag cool, never directly on ice. Bring it with you and reach a hand surgeon immediately.

Can I just visit a general clinic first?

For minor cuts and sprains, yes. But deep cuts, numbness, deformity or loss of movement deserve a hand specialist directly; every transfer between clinics costs time that injured tendons and nerves do not have.

How should I transport someone with a hand injury?

Keep the hand elevated and immobilised, maintain pressure on any bleeding, and avoid unnecessary movement of injured fingers. Do not let the person drive themselves if the dominant hand is injured or they feel faint.
