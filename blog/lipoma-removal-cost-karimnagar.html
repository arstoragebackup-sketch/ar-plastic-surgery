How lipoma removal pricing works

A lipoma is a soft, slow-growing lump of fatty tissue under the skin. Removal is a minor surgical procedure: the lump is removed through a small incision, usually under local anaesthesia, and the wound is closed. Pricing follows the lump itself — its size, its depth, how many there are, and where on the body they sit — not a single flat fee for every case.

That is why two people can pay different totals for lipoma removal at the same clinic. One small lump on the arm is a short, straightforward procedure. A large or deep lump, or several lumps removed together, takes longer and needs more careful work, so the total is higher. The quote is set after the surgeon has examined the lump or lumps — not from a description over the phone.

6 factors that decide your price

  1. Size of the lipoma. The single biggest factor — larger lumps take longer to remove.
  2. Number of lipomas. Each additional lipoma removed adds to the total.
  3. Depth and location. Deep lipomas, and lipomas near nerves, vessels or joints, need more careful dissection.
  4. Anaesthesia and procedure time. Longer procedures and the anaesthesia used sit inside the quote.
  5. Who performs the surgery. A qualified, experienced surgeon's assessment and technique are part of what you pay for.
  6. Follow-up and wound care. Whether dressing changes and review visits are included in the quote.

What's usually included — and what to ask

A proper written quote should itemise: the consultation and examination, the procedure itself, anaesthesia, dressings, and scheduled review visits. Before you commit at any clinic, ask:

  • Who will perform the surgery — the doctor or someone else?
  • How many lipomas are included in this quote?
  • What anaesthesia will be used?
  • Is the removed tissue sent for examination, and is that included?
  • Are dressing changes and review visits included?
  • Can I see the quote in writing before the procedure?

Red flags in very low quotes

If a quote is far below the market range, slow down and check: is a qualified doctor actually performing the surgery, or is it delegated? Does the quote cover all the lipomas discussed, or will extra lumps appear as extra charges later? Is tissue examination, dressing care and review included at all? Price matters, but this is surgery — the cheapest option is not automatically the safest one.
